Copy and paste breaks your data. This doesn't.

A table pasted into Excel looks fine until you try to use it. These are the things Export Button fixes on its own, before the file reaches you.

Numbers stay numbers

Amounts with currency symbols and thousands separators become real numbers, in any format: $1,234.56, 1.234,56 €, R$ 1.234,56. The separator is worked out from the whole column.

$162,700pasted as text→162,700.00a number you can sum

Dates become dates

10/14/2026, 14/10/2026, Oct 14, 2026: day and month order is read from the column, so dates sort and filter the way they should.

Oct 14, 2026text that won't sort→10/14/2026a real Excel date

Codes keep their zeros

ZIP codes, invoice numbers, tax IDs and phone numbers stay exactly as written. No more 02134 turning into 2134.

2134what Excel does→02134kept as written

The tables other tools miss

Tables inside iframes, a frozen first column next to the scrolling ones, headers kept in a separate table, data split across many pages. It finds them and puts them back together.

2 tablesnames here, figures there→1 sheetnames and figures together

Three steps, nothing to set up

Pin the icon

After installing, click the puzzle piece at the top right of the browser and pin Export Button, so it's always one click away.

Open the page with your table

A company portal, an old ERP screen, a report, a public page. Click the icon: the tables are listed, and each one lights up on the page as you hover it.

Choose Excel or CSV

The file is built in your browser and downloaded, already formatted with filters on. Open it and get to work.

Your office data stays in your browser

Export Button reads the table and builds the file on your computer. No servers, no account, no tracking. That's why it's safe on internal portals too.
