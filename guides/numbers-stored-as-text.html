Numbers pasted from a website become text in Excel

The table looked fine on the page. In Excel the amounts sit on the left, there's a small green triangle in the corner, and SUM returns 0. Or worse: 1,234 has become 1.234. Here's why, and how to fix it.

Three different problems

On the pageIn ExcelWhat happened
$1,250.00'$1,250.00 (text)The currency symbol or a stray space stopped Excel from reading a number
1.250,001.25 or textThe site uses a comma for decimals, your Excel uses a dot (or the other way round)
10/12/202610 DecemberThe site writes month/day, your Excel reads day/month

The first one is easy to spot. The second and third are dangerous, because Excel doesn't complain: it shows a number, just the wrong one.

Fix 1: Convert to Number

If the cells show a green triangle, select the whole column, click the warning icon that appears next to the selection and choose Convert to Number. It works when the text is a clean number in your own format. It doesn't help when there's a currency symbol in the way or the separators are the other way round.

Fix 2: Text to Columns, with the right separators

This is the most reliable fix for amounts written in another country's format.

  1. Select the column.
  2. Data › Text to Columns › Delimited › Next, untick every delimiter › Next.
  3. Click Advanced… and set the Decimal separator and Thousands separator the way the website writes them (for 1.250,00: decimal “,” and thousands “.”).
  4. Leave the column format on General and click Finish.

In the same last step you can fix dates too: choose Date and the order the site uses (MDY or DMY).

Fix 3: the NUMBERVALUE formula

When you'd rather not touch the original column, add a new one with:

=NUMBERVALUE(A2, ",", ".")

The second argument is the decimal separator used in the text, the third is the thousands separator. Remove currency symbols first, for example =NUMBERVALUE(SUBSTITUTE(A2,"€",""), ",", "."). Then copy the column and paste it back as values.

Fix 4: Find and Replace

For a currency symbol or a non-breaking space that sticks to every value, Home › Find & Select › Replace (Ctrl+H), replace the symbol with nothing, then use Fix 1. Careful with dots and commas: replacing them by hand on a column of mixed values is how 1.5 becomes 15.

Check before you trust a total. After any fix, compare two or three values with the page, including one with decimals and one above a thousand. An amount that's off by a factor of a thousand looks perfectly normal in a column.
